Cotopaxi is 5,897 meters, a classic cone south of Quito, and one of the highest active volcanoes on earth. The 2015 eruption closed the park and reminded the valley that the postcard is also a mountain. It is open again. It is still active.

## The park, not the summit

Most visitors come from [Quito](/visit/quito/) for the day. The aim is Laguna Limpiopungo, a high plain of páramo, and the view of the cone when cloud allows it. Wild horses and carunculated caracaras are the usual company. The mountain is often hidden until late morning, then gone again. Patience is the technique.

The José Ribas refuge sits near 4,800 meters. The walk up from the parking area is the standard non-technical hike: steep, sandy, and short enough to underestimate. At that altitude, “short” is not the same as easy. Turn around if your head or your stomach argues.

## The ice

The summit is a glaciated climb. It starts in the middle of the night from the refuge, uses crampons and a rope, and belongs with an accredited mountain guide. People do it with a day of training. People also get into trouble doing that. If you are not already comfortable on a glacier, do not make Cotopaxi the place you learn. The [hiking chapter](/do/hiking/) says the same about Chimborazo.

Park rules and any entrance fee should be checked before you go. They change. So does the road after rain.

## How to go

A guided day is the simple version: someone else watches the weather and the time. A car gives you Limpiopungo at your own pace, and nothing above the refuge. Acclimatize in Quito first. This is a poor plan for the morning after a night flight.

Pair it with [Quilotoa](/visit/quilotoa/) if you have a second day and the lungs for more altitude. They are different landscapes and the same lesson.

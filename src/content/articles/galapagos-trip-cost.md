A total for “a Galápagos trip” would be a fiction. Cruises, land weeks, and last-minute flights are not one number. Two fees are published. The rest you ask for.

<div class="facts cols-2">
<p><strong>$200</strong><span>Park conservation fee for a foreign tourist over 12. Under 12: $100. From the park’s current table.</span></p>
<p><strong>$20</strong><span>Transit control card, for foreigners and Ecuadorians. Free under 2. Paid apart from the park fee.</span></p>
</div>

## The park fee

The Dirección del Parque Nacional Galápagos collects the entry tribute for the protected areas. Park rangers collect it at the airports on Baltra and San Cristóbal. The table on the park’s site is headed as provincial ordinance 001-CGREG-30-06-2025.

The park’s page says children under 2, and people entering as transients, are exempt. A US tourist over 12 is the $200 line. The United States is not in the Andean Community or Mercosur. Those lists on the page are Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru, and Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay.

## The transit card

The Consejo de Gobierno’s procedure says every tourist or transient needs a tarjeta de control de tránsito. The price it prints is $20 for foreigners and nationals, and nothing for children under 2.

You pre-register online, then show documents at the CGREG counter in Quito or Guayaquil airport and pay. The legal note on that page asks for identification, and for a personal round-trip ticket between the mainland and the islands. Tourists, on that same page, are people visiting for leisure for at most two months in the year.

The UK entry page adds, for the islands, evidence of a hotel or a host letter and travel health insurance for foreign tourists. That insurance sentence is the FCDO’s. Confirm it before you treat it as a rule on a US passport.

## What this page will not price

- The flight from Quito or Guayaquil to Baltra or San Cristóbal
- A hotel in Puerto Ayora, a cabin on a cruise, or a day boat
- Meals, tips, or a wetsuit

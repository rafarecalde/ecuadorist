Rent in Ecuador is a local agreement, usually on paper, almost always in Spanish. The foreigners who have a bad first year are the ones who wired a deposit from abroad to a listing they liked.

## Start short

Use a hotel or a short let for the first weeks. Walk the neighborhood in the evening. Time the trip to a market and to the clinic. The [cities chapter](/retire/best-cities/) is a list of candidates, not a verdict. A booking site is for those first nights. It is not how you sign a year’s lease.

Furnished apartments are common and, for a newcomer, sane. You can buy furniture later, when you know the city.

## The contract

Have a bilingual person read the lease before you sign. Ask about the deposit, the notice period, who pays building fees, and what happens to the deposit at the end. Do not accept “everyone does it this way” as a translation.

Utilities are straightforward in cities and less so on the edge of them. Ask whether the building has a cistern. Water pressure fails in places, and a tank is the local answer. Hot water is often an on-demand gas heater, not a tank in the basement. Internet in Quito and Cuenca is routinely fiber, from providers such as Netlife or CNT. Confirm it in the specific building.

## Neighborhood, not postcard

In [Cuenca](/visit/cuenca/), the historic center and the western neighborhoods draw different lives. In [Quito](/visit/quito/), the center, La Floresta, La Carolina, and the valley towns are not interchangeable. Visit them. A pretty balcony over a bus terminal is still a bus terminal.

## Money

We will not quote a rent. It moves, and a single number would be a fiction. Pay through a method you can document. Meet the landlord or the agent at the apartment. The [banking chapter](/retire/banking/) covers how dollars actually move. If someone asks you to send a deposit before you land, the answer is no.

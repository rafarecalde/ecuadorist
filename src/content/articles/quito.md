Quito sits near 2,850 meters, about 9,350 feet, in a long Andean valley. The historic center has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1978. The rest of the city is offices, parks, and traffic. Both are the point.

## The old town

Plaza Grande, formally Plaza de la Independencia, is the civic center: cathedral, presidential palace, and a square that still behaves like one. A short walk downhill, Plaza de San Francisco opens onto a wide church facade, with the Virgin of El Panecillo on the hill behind it.

La Compañía de Jesús is among the most ornate churches in the Americas. The Basílica del Voto Nacional is the Gothic landmark you see from across the valley; visitors can climb its towers when the church allows it. Do not plan the day around a single opening hour. Ask when you arrive.

The streets reward a slow loop more than a checklist. Mornings are kinder than late afternoons, when clouds often sit on Pichincha.

## The newer city

North of the old town, La Mariscal and La Floresta are where much of the newer restaurant scene lives. Parque La Carolina is the big green space. Guápulo, on the eastern edge, drops into a gorge and feels like another town.

TelefériQo, the cable car, rises to Cruz Loma on Pichincha. The view is the reason to go. The air up there is thinner than in the plaza, and the weather turns quickly.

The Mitad del Mundo monument commemorates the eighteenth-century expedition that measured the equator. Modern GPS puts the line a short distance from the monument. Treat it as a piece of history with a good story, not as a scientific instrument.

## The first day

Altitude is the only urgent advice. Walk, drink water, go easy on wine, and do not book Cotopaxi for the morning you land. Some people feel nothing. Some feel a headache and a short temper. Both are normal.

Mariscal Sucre Airport is on the eastern plateau, not in the city. A registered taxi or a prearranged car is the sensible arrival. The people behind this site run [UIO Transfers](https://uiotransfers.com) for that ride. It is their company, not a third-party ad.

Use ordinary city sense. Crowded streets and bus terminals are where phones disappear. The old town in the evening is atmospheric; stick to busy streets.

## Beyond the plaza

Quito is a base, not the whole trip. [Cotopaxi](/visit/cotopaxi/) is a day. [Mindo](/visit/mindo/) is a night in the cloud forest. [Otavalo](/visit/otavalo/) is a Saturday. The road east toward Papallacta is the way to hot water and, much farther on, the Amazon.

Booked city days and the monument are on [Mitad del Mundo and Quito city tours](/book/quito-city-and-mitad-del-mundo-tours/). A car at the terminal is [renting a car at Quito airport](/book/quito-airport-car-rental/). Data before you land is the [eSIM for Ecuador](/book/ecuador-esim/).

Spanish changes the city. English is easy to find in hotels and some dining rooms, and scarce in markets and offices.

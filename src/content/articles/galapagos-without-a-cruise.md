A land trip is a town, a bed, and a boat that comes back the same day. It is not a lesser cruise. It is a different map.

<div class="facts cols-3">
<p><strong>Santa Cruz</strong><span>Puerto Ayora. The busy base. Baltra airport is a crossing away.</span></p>
<p><strong>San Cristóbal</strong><span>Puerto Baquerizo Moreno. The capital. The airport is on the island.</span></p>
<p><strong>Isabela</strong><span>Puerto Villamil. The large, slow island. Usually a boat, not a jet.</span></p>
</div>

## How the hops work

Flights from Quito and Guayaquil land at Baltra, which serves Santa Cruz, or at San Cristóbal. That choice is the first hop, and it is the one to get right. The [island comparison](/visit/which-galapagos-island/) is which town you actually want to wake up in.

Between the inhabited islands, people use boats. This guide does not print a timetable or a fare. Both change, and a copied schedule is how someone misses the last boat. The FCDO’s warning is the part that does not change with the season: ask who operates the boat, whether it is licensed for the run, and whether there is a life vest for you. Day tours that leave Santa Cruz for a visitor site are the same question. The [Santa Cruz day-tour page](/book/galapagos-day-tours-santa-cruz/) is a set of booked examples, not a ferry board.

Visitor sites in the national park are not a walk you invent. The [Galápagos chapter](/visit/galapagos/) is the rule: marked trails, distance from the animals, a guide when the site requires one. A blue-footed booby that ignores you is the point.

## A week without a ship

<ol class="route">
<li><strong>Night 0</strong><span>Transit card at the Quito or Guayaquil airport, before the island flight. The <a href="/visit/galapagos-trip-cost/">cost chapter</a> has the $20 figure and the park fee.</span></li>
<li><strong>Nights 1–4</strong><span>One base. Santa Cruz if you want the most day boats. San Cristóbal if you want the airport in town and a quieter capital.</span></li>
<li><strong>Nights 5–6</strong><span>A second island, if the boat is one you would put your family on. Isabela is the usual addition. A third island in six nights is how luggage wins.</span></li>
<li><strong>Last morning</strong><span>Back to the airport island you can actually fly from. Do not finish on Isabela the morning of a mainland connection unless the boat and the flight were sold as one plan.</span></li>
</ol>

The [two-week route](/visit/two-weeks-ecuador-galapagos/) is this week with the Andes in front of it. A cruise is the other product: more sites, less town, a cabin you do not choose each night. Neither price belongs on this page.

Quito eats in two registers. The historic center still does soup, hornado, and a view. North of it, a smaller group of kitchens has spent the last decade taking Ecuadorian ingredients seriously. Both are worth your time. Neither comes with a guarantee that a specific dining room is open on the night you land.

## A short list

These names have shaped how people talk about food in the city. Confirm they are operating. We are not listing hours, prices, or addresses.

- **Nuema** and **Urko** are part of the modern Andean dining that put Quito on serious lists.
- **Café Mosaico** is the long-running room associated with the view over the old town.
- **Casa Gangotena**, the hotel on Plaza de San Francisco, has a dining room equal to the building.

That is a short list on purpose. A longer one would start inventing.

## Neighborhoods

In the Centro Histórico, eat during the day and walk it off. The plazas are the landmarks, not a recommendation of any one stall. Mercado Central is the market for a bowl and a look at what the city actually buys.

La Mariscal is denser with visitors. La Floresta is where a lot of the newer, smaller rooms have opened. Both reward a wander more than a single booking.

The dishes themselves are in [what to eat](/eat/ecuadorian-food/). If you only have one meal and you want the country rather than a scene, take the set lunch.

## Getting there hungry

Mariscal Sucre is not a quick hop from the old town. Eat after you have dropped your bags, not on the way from the curb. A prearranged car, including the owner’s own [UIO Transfers](https://uiotransfers.com), is the calm version of arrival. Then use the [Quito chapter](/visit/quito/) for the rest of the city.
